# Iași's Piața Unirii camera anchors the demo

The best camera for an Iași traffic-incident detection demo is the **WebcamRomania.ro feed over Piața Unirii, hosted by Grand Hotel Traian**. It is a fixed, elevated, wide-angle view of a multi-lane boulevard with a tram line, a taxi rank and pedestrian crossings. It streams as **unauthenticated HLS at 1920x1080, 20 fps** (`https://w1.webcamromania.ro/piatauniriiiasi/index.m3u8`), and ffmpeg or OpenCV opens it with no Referer or token ([WebcamRomania](https://webcamromania.ro/webcam-orase/webcam-iasi/)). Two more Iași-county road feeds work the same way: a Digi (RCS&RDS) overhead camera near the Palace of Culture (1280x800, 30 fps) and Gara Pașcani (1920x1080, 30 fps). Elsewhere in Romania, WebcamRomania's HLS network adds strong arterial views in Ploiești, Bucharest Piața Romană, the Giroc roundabout near Timișoara and DN1 at Predeal. Official Romanian sources offer nothing usable. CNAIR's "Trafic live" cameras run on a dead 2016 Flash/RTMP server, and Iași's 88-intersection traffic-management CCTV feeds a closed control centre. The catch is licensing: **no Romanian camera carries an open licence**, so these feeds suit a private, non-commercial prototype that credits its sources, not a public product. Openly licensed footage exists abroad but trades away frame rate. **TfL JamCams (London) are the only openly licensed European source with moving video**, and they deliver only 10-second 352x288 clips. Caltrans offers about 2,300 keyless HLS streams, but its current terms could not be retrieved. Every probe in the research notes was run on 2026-10-20, and no public RTSP or MJPEG traffic feed turned up anywhere.

## Three Iași feeds stream raw HLS, and Piața Unirii is the clear first choice

Iași has no official public traffic camera. The city finished an integrated traffic-management system in 2016, covering 1,242 traffic lights at 88 intersections, and its cameras serve a dispatch centre and police investigators rather than the public ([BZI](https://www.bzi.ro/sistemul-de-semaforizare-din-iasi-se-confrunta-cu-multiple-provocari-foto-4686264); [BZI](https://www.bzi.ro/locul-secret-de-unde-sunt-verificati-iesenii-toti-anchetatorii-preiau-imaginile-accidentele-rutiere-sunt-surprinse-secunda-cu-secunda-video-in-premiera-4129065)). Getting those feeds would take a formal data-sharing agreement with the Traffic Management Office (Biroul Management Trafic) or the Local Police (Poliția Locală). Local media and YouTube offer no substitute: a YouTube live search for Iași on 2026-10-20 returned only a cathedral service stream ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=iasi+live&sp=EgJAAQ%253D%253D)). The usable feeds therefore come from private webcam operators.

Piața Unirii ranks first because the vehicles are large in frame and the scene packs in the interactions an incident model needs: taxis pulling in and out, trams, crossings and parked cars. The Digi camera has a higher frame rate but a worse angle. Its steep downward view shrinks vehicles and trees partly hide the far lanes. It is mirrored on Windy as webcam 1465668398 and re-streamed by WebcamRomania at `https://w3.webcamromania.ro/ispalatulculturii/index.m3u8`. Windy's JPEG snapshot refreshes only about every 50 minutes, which is useless for detection ([Windy](https://www.windy.com/webcams/1465668398); [WebcamRomania](https://webcamromania.ro/webcam-orase/webcam-iasi-palatul-culturii/)). Gara Pașcani is a good second scene, combining a station forecourt with pedestrians and taxis. Two listed sources are not worth chasing. The Vama Sculeni border-crossing camera was offline, and its embed host did not respond ([WebcamRomania](https://webcamromania.ro/webcam-orase/webcam-vama-sculeni/)). The Aeroclubul Iași stream on freecam.ro is live at 2560x1440 but shows an airfield, not a road ([freecam.ro](https://www.freecam.ro/aerodromuri/aerodromul-iasi)).

The research found **no public camera on DN28, DN24, the Iași ring road (Centura Iași) or the A8 construction sites**. Road reporting there cites DRDP Iași bulletins, not webcams ([BZI](https://www.bzi.ro/accident-pe-dn-28-la-limita-judetului-iasi-cu-strunga-un-tir-a-fost-implicat-5546976)). Night-time image quality, uptime history and exact camera bearings were not measured for any Iași feed.

The edge hostnames also move between w1, w2 and w3, so a client should re-scrape each page for its current `.m3u8` instead of hard-coding the URL.

## Romanian feeds are technically open but legally unlicensed

Being easy to ingest does not make a feed licensed. **WebcamRomania states that the streams belong to their original owners and are shown by embed.** Its terms forbid aggressive automated scraping (*scrape automat agresiv*) and rate-limit bypass, and they require the administrator's written consent to reproduce site content. They say nothing on recording or computer-vision processing by users ([WebcamRomania Termeni](https://webcamromania.ro/termeni-si-conditii/)). The Digi page carries a DIGI Romania copyright footer, and its webcam-specific terms could not be retrieved ([digi.ro](https://www.digi.ro/servicii/online/web-cams)). **SkylineWebcams is explicit**: viewers may not "download, extract, photograph, print or make copies of any Content," and redistribution needs written authorisation from VisioRay ([Skyline Terms](https://www.skylinewebcams.com/en/terms-of-use.html)). Many of Skyline's Romanian pages simply wrap a YouTube stream, so the YouTube owner is the party that matters.

YouTube creates a separate problem. `yt-dlp -g` or streamlink will resolve a live stream to its HLS manifest, but YouTube's Terms of Service bar access by "automated means" without prior written permission ([YouTube Terms](https://www.youtube.com/terms)). The Bârlad, Codlea and Făgăraș streams are therefore weaker legal footing than WebcamRomania's direct HLS, even though they are technically fine.

For a private, non-commercial prototype that samples frames at a modest rate, does not redistribute footage and credits its sources (WebcamRomania.ro / Grand Hotel Traian; DIGI Romania), the Romanian HLS feeds are a reasonable working choice. That is a judgement on the risk, not a licence. A public demo, or anything that records or republishes frames, should first get written permission from the camera owner. Many owners are reachable: Ilișești's camera is branded by its city hall, Codlea's stream is run by its city hall, and the rest can be contacted through WebcamRomania's contact form.

## Openly licensed feeds abroad trade frame rate for clean terms

If clean reuse rights matter more than location, the options narrow quickly. Across all the European government sources reviewed, **no one openly offers continuous live video**. Only the US and London have programmatic video at scale.

**TfL JamCams is the best openly licensed fit for a video demo.** The 10-second clips at 25 fps are enough for short-window tracking, and polling all 890 cameras yields a steady stream of fresh fixed-angle road footage ([TfL API](https://api.tfl.gov.uk/Place/Type/JamCam)). Its weakness is resolution: at CIF (352x288), small or distant vehicles will detect poorly, and the clips are not continuous, so an incident that happens between clips is missed.

**Caltrans is the strongest continuous-video source on any continent.** One importer reports 2,304 cameras, each with a direct `.m3u8`, and recommends a full ffmpeg build because some streams fail under certain OpenCV builds ([camai-saas PR #4](https://github.com/zaptapagency/camai-saas/pull/4)). Three caveats limit it. The backends are flaky, and one load-balancer IP returns spurious 403s ([cooltrans](https://github.com/alexmac/cooltrans)). The hosts refused connections from the research machine, which suggests they block by IP or region, so reachability must be tested from the deployment host. And the governing terms are unconfirmed: the only text recovered is a 1997-era memo restricting use to traveller information and barring plate disclosure ([Caltrans memo](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/traffic-operations/documents/memos-letters/97-1-topd-a11y.pdf)).

Digitraffic has the cleanest licence, but its 10-minute cadence means it can show a scene before and after an incident, never the incident itself ([Digitraffic history](https://tie.digitraffic.fi/api/weathercam/v1/stations/C01503/history)).

Several other sources do not fit. US 511 platforms (GA, AZ, NY) document only keyed snapshot APIs throttled to 10 calls per 60 seconds ([511GA](https://511ga.org/developers/doc)). FL511 exposes HLS in its tooltip HTML, but direct requests get a 401 without a token. NYC DOT serves stills refreshed about every 2 seconds ([NBC New York](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/local/how-to-take-selfies-with-new-york-citys-traffic-cameras/5698806/)), and WSDOT's API is snapshots only ([WSDOT](https://wsdot.wa.gov/Traffic/api/Documentation/class_highway_cameras.html)). Germany's Autobahn API still returns an empty webcam list after the March 2022 shutdown ([Autobahn API](https://verkehr.autobahn.de/o/autobahn/A1/services/webcam); [PC-Welt](https://www.pcwelt.de/article/1202790/sicherheitsgruende-keine-bilder-mehr-von-deutschen-autobahn-webcams.html)). National Highways (England) images are Crown Copyright, and reuse beyond non-commercial research needs a bespoke licence ([National Highways](https://nationalhighways.co.uk/travel-updates/traffic-cameracctv-services/standard-terms-and-conditions/)). EarthCam licenses its streams commercially to media under credit conditions ([EarthCam](https://cvps.earthcam.com/mediapriorityaccess)). The Netherlands, Poland, Hungary, Bulgaria, Moldova, Italy and Switzerland were not researched.

## Conclusion

The trade-off is sharper than expected: **the feeds that best suit incident detection have no clear reuse rights, and the openly licensed ones are too slow or too low-resolution to catch an incident as it happens**. In practice, build the Iași demo on Piața Unirii's 1080p HLS, with Gara Pașcani and Ploiești as backups. Treat permission from Grand Hotel Traian or WebcamRomania as the gate before any public showing. Use TfL JamCams as the openly licensed control set that can be shown anywhere. One finding matters for the longer term: the Iași traffic-management system already watches 88 intersections around the clock, so a working prototype on a hotel webcam is the strongest case for a data-sharing agreement with the city. That agreement, not a better public webcam, is what would give the product real incident coverage.

The biggest open questions before committing are night-time and rain performance on the Iași feeds, which nobody measured; Caltrans' resolution, frame rate and current terms, which need probing from a US host; and whether the camera owners will agree to recording. Each of these takes about a day to resolve, and none needs new research infrastructure.
